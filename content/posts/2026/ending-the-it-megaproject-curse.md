This week the [Institute for Research on Public Policy](https://irpp.org/) (IRPP) [published a memo that I wrote](https://irpp.org/research-studies/ending-it-megaproject-curse/) as part of their series on state capacity. 

The series is titled *[Capacity for Change: Designing a Public Service Built to Deliver](https://irpp.org/capacity-for-change/)* and I’m thrilled that the IRPP has put together a focused look at how to improve state capacity in Canada. State capacity has been [on](/2022/05/17/everything-is-broken-and-no-one-seems-to-mind/) [my](/2023/09/26/coasian-hecks-or-when-the-people-in-charge-cant-change-things-either/) [mind](/2023/11/06/to-the-clerk-and-friends/) for some time – essentially, are our public service institutions able to effectively deliver on the priorities of our elected leaders and the needs of Canadians? (Often: [no](/2023/11/06/revolution-not-evolution-for-federal-public-service-delivery/).) 

The memo I wrote is focused on government technology capacity and how to improve it, building on [some of the earlier research that I did](/2024/10/03/published-in-canadian-public-administration-breaking-all-the-rules-information-technology-procurement-in-the-government-of-canada/) with Prof. [Amanda Clarke](https://amandaclarke.ca/). (Prof. Clarke also co-wrote a memo that is part of the same series – [Dismantling Ottawa’s Costly Web of Rules: How to Build a Functioning Federal Accountability Regime](https://irpp.org/research-studies/functioning-federal-accountability-regime/). It’s a must-read.)

In this memo, I look at how past technology modernization efforts have gone sideways – mostly, ill-fated megaprojects – and how we could deliver government technology systems that actually work.

> Delivering technology projects in a smaller cost and time range is doable. The added benefit is that technology systems — and the services to the public that depend on them — can be delivered *quickly enough to be relevant* for the policy issues and priorities of the day. A project development period of five to 10 years does not help governments respond to the urgent needs of a pandemic, a large-scale disaster, an economic crisis, or geopolitical and military situations. Small, inexpensive and iterative technology implementation is *useful* technology implementation. The alternative is our current norm: outdated and mediocre, if a system even launches at all.

You can [read the full memo on the IRPP’s Capacity for Change website](https://irpp.org/research-studies/ending-it-megaproject-curse/) (or [print off a PDF copy](https://irpp.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Ending-the-IT-megaproject-curse.pdf) for your favourite paper-based senior government executive!).
